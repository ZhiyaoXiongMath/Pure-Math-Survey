# Reader capabilities and a walkable mathematical route

The default is general undergraduate mathematics plus early doctoral background in the
relevant neighboring field, not familiarity with this particular module. Treat this as
a declared, revisable writing assumption, not a measured reader state. Do not combine
every doctoral curriculum into one imaginary reader.

State in `scope.md` what the reader can do with the tools used here; refine the output's
`audience`, `reader_goals`, `prerequisites` and `outline.md` as needed. “Knows compactness”
is insufficient: specify the bounded set, topology, subsequence conclusion and needed
conditions. “Knows linear algebra” might mean testing positivity by a quadratic form,
not knowing Schur complements.

For the current use, distinguish **Assumed** (call directly, with local notation),
**Refreshable** (give the exact usable version and input/output), and **Bridge-required**
(build by definition, model or calculation before use). These are editorial roles,
not permanent difficulty labels in the nodes. Recognizing a name, applying a theorem
and proving it are different capabilities.

Work backward from the exact result: what intermediate object/property must be
supplied? Then read forward from this reader: can they understand why the object is
introduced, verify the next input and recognize the output? Where the routes do not
meet, supply a reminder, model or construction. If the mathematical implication itself
is missing, return to knowledge and review, not to more confident prose.

A point is large enough to be useful and small enough to be callable. Delete a proposed
point as a test: if nothing becomes harder, it may be irrelevant. If retaining it still
does not explain its role, refine it. Stop when the genuine main route can be followed,
not after a fixed number of points or pages.

Fast understanding exposes objects, useful inputs, decisive choices and valid
connections. Slow understanding fills the calculations and technical lemmas on that
same route. Uniformity, branch selection, admissibility, noncollapse and regularity
belong in the fast layer whenever they decide whether the route works. An open cone
need not contain the limit of points in that cone. A weak limit does not automatically
pass a nonlinear equation. Never hide these questions under “technical details.”

During author rereading, reconstruct the target, points, conversions and conditions
without looking at long proof interiors, then verify that the interiors support exactly
that skeleton. Locate failures rather than score comprehension. This is author self-check,
not an independent reviewer or a real-reader experiment.

## Required reader tasks before acceptance

Keep this capability baseline throughout revision; do not silently raise it after
finding explanations hard. Before checking positions, reconstruct the necessary
obligations independently of the writer's step list. Then save actual fast-route
answers, one or more promised slow derivations and the first failure after changing
a decisive condition, including missing input, repair and reattempt. This applies to
the KB itself without articles. Nodes/routes use specific subsection entrances and
fixed input hashes; output findings use actual body locations. Team/AI attempts are
named as such and cannot be reported as target-student feedback.
