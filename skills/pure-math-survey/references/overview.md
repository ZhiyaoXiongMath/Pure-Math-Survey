# Project overview versus one article's outline

`knowledge/overview.md` is readable mathematical organization for the project. It can
cover several questions, distinct routes, genuine branches and unresolved steps. It is
not a file directory, paper-by-paper abstract list, chronology or printed dependency
graph. It is not a common introduction to copy into all articles, and not Minimal's
third section.

The reader should identify the mathematical objects, data and unknowns, accurate targets,
callable tools, purpose of intermediate constructions, decisive conditions, actual gaps
and precise entrances into detail. These are reading functions, not six compulsory
headings or a per-node card format. Use formulas when they make the current object or
operation visible. Define unfamiliar notation locally.

Proof dependencies answer which premises a claim needs. Teaching order answers what
a reader should see first to understand why the next construction is natural. They
need not have the same order. Joint premises must be shown as joint; alternatives
must not be stacked as if all were necessary or as if each were separately sufficient.

Bad: “Use compactness, then regularity; details are in the knowledge base.”
Better: identify the uniform `C^{2,alpha}` bound, strong `C^{2,beta}` subsequence,
continuity of the operator in that topology, and the uniform positivity margin that
keeps the limit admissible. Link the exact node and subsection proving each interface.
Do not assert that those estimates exist unless they have been established.

Nodes retain the single authority for exact definitions, results and relations. An
overview may explain a claim but may not silently enlarge its quantifiers, branch or
status. New implications or comparisons discovered while writing must become reviewed
relation/result/mechanism nodes before formal use. Canonical statements are imported
from a fixed snapshot wherever a complete formal statement is needed.

`outputs/ID/outline.md` instead selects this article's reader task, principals, points,
connections, imported inputs, promised detail and mode-specific sequence. It need not
revisit unrelated project branches, but must not omit dependencies actually used.
Internal `[[N-...]]` links aid tracing; node IDs and hashes are not the reader-facing
mathematical narrative.

Shared overview/scope files retain file-level consumption and invalidation. There is
no paragraph-level change analysis, new graph schema or second knowledge authority.
Preparing again preserves authored outlines and manuscripts. A revised mathematical
statement requires a new snapshot, not an output-only correction.

## Knowledge-only fast and slow routes

Use three scales: project overview, problem route and exact node. They are reading
functions, not mandatory separate trees. Even a knowledge-only request must deliver
usable fast routes, reproducible slow details and precise bidirectional entrances.
For every nontrivial arrow identify the actual input, operation, obstacle removed,
output and decisive conditions; link the specific supporting node/subsection. Each
slow subsection names the route it discharges and where its output is consumed.

The compressed unit is a construction already made callable for this reader. Naming
an unfamiliar tensor or writing a determinant without its object-to-density explanation
does not make it usable. Slow passages supply promised derivations, conditions,
endpoint checks, constant dependence, exact imports and their present application.
Do not hide gaps as technical details or draw joint premises as individually sufficient.

Hide long interiors and reconstruct the fast route, perform a slow step, then change a
condition and locate the first failure. Save actual attempts and fixes against fixed
node/route inputs, even without any article. Reusable explanations discovered during
writing return to the live KB, receive review and a new snapshot before output adoption.
Pure transitions/layout remain in the article. Keep file-level invalidation; do not
claim paragraph-level dependency analysis or a second canonical authority.
