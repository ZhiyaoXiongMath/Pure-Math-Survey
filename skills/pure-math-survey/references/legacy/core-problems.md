# Core questions, frontier discovery and selection

The goal is to find what a knowledgeable reader ought not to miss, not merely to check whether already selected questions remain open. This is an editorial research obligation, not a Problem quota. A field with a rich solved theory may still have a central unresolved structural question.

## Discover before freezing the bibliography

Start with the definitions and variation analysis in `problem-formulation.md`. Reconstruct the domain's own original aims, not only the aims of the papers already supplied. Read primary introductions and concluding discussions; trace an explicit question to its original formulation. Use current papers and alternative names to search beyond that initial chain. A user's suggested famous neighboring problem is a candidate, not automatically the organizing frontier.

For each important direction ask: what is already characterized or constructed; what stronger, more intrinsic, more effective or more explanatory answer is being sought; what is the exact gap? An existence criterion need not explain destabilizing objects, finite detection, moduli, degenerations or an expected structural correspondence. These are examples of gap types, not a compulsory menu. Do not presuppose a particular stability theory, technique, author or conjecture.

Use searches derived from the question and gap, not just paper titles or names in the current bibliography. Follow citations forward as well as backward, checking proofs, counterexamples, corrections, restricted cases and changed formulations. Record discoveries that change scope even when the associated paper will not be printed. Reading a chosen URL is a `source_read`, not a `discovery_search` or `followup_search`. Record exact executed queries, dates, platforms, retrieved findings and saved evidence. Irrelevant or empty search output is a limitation requiring refinement, not evidence of openness.

## Mathematical status and publication disposition

Use `SOLVED`, `REFUTED`, `VERIFIED_OPEN`, or `STATUS_UNVERIFIED` for an exact mathematical claim. Use `INCLUDE`, `CONTEXT`, or `OMIT` independently for this article's treatment. `OUT_OF_SCOPE` is a legacy editorial value, not a mathematical status. Migrate it to a disposition while preserving the evidence; absent verification, retain `STATUS_UNVERIFIED`, never invent a status.

Use `precise-question`, `research-direction`, or `refuted-formulation` for the kind of candidate. A research direction may be important without being a fully specified conjecture; it must not masquerade as a formal Problem with a truth-status certificate. An exact question requires objects, range, target and source. Partial progress is recorded as `known_range` and `remaining_target`; do not label the original full statement solved merely because a special case is known.

`VERIFIED_OPEN` means a source-authenticated unresolved target, with a current follow-up search that did not reveal a resolution within the recorded scope. It is not a proof that no resolution exists. A newer preprint is identified at a version and distinguished from a published theorem. Failure to obtain a primary statement is a stated verification limitation. An already refuted formulation belongs with its counterexample, never among open conjectures.

A counterexample to an unrestricted correspondence does not erase established restricted or generic equivalences. Check the adjacent remarks, corollaries and known parameter chambers; state the exact extra hypothesis that restores the implication and the boundary where it fails. Do not invent a genericity condition, conflate a class variation with a representative change, or call a solved restricted range open. Record this in the existing `known_range` and `remaining_target`, not another checklist.

## Select, then challenge omissions

Keep the article centered on its own problem. Explain each important candidate's relevance and disposition. A related grand conjecture is context unless it actually answers or organizes the selected question. Boundary results receive space only when they clarify an asserted limit; they are not an automatic section quota.

Before release, reread the relevant primary introductions without first consulting the selection registry. Reconstruct the domain-native frontier and compare it to the article. Ask which major question was never admitted to the candidate universe and whether selection of a favorite method displaced the real problem. Record the actual discrepancy found or the source-based reason no material omission was found. This may be the author's source-first reread, but must not be called independent review.

Stop when the important scope obligations have a documented disposition and targeted follow-up searches no longer change the selected claims or reveal a material missing direction. Record unresolved search/access limits. Do not claim exhaustive coverage or keep searching to reach a reference count. With genuinely no verified open question, publish an accurate status account and no active Problem environment; justify this against the discovery evidence. Zero Problems is not the same as zero research.

## Bind publication to evidence

Use the existing `frontier-claim-registry.csv`, `frontier-reverse-search.csv`, `publication-map.csv` and `survey_selection` inside each document's existing structure review. Bind included frontier claims to meaningful source spans, not just labels or author keywords. Retain current source locators and mathematical limitations. A changed manuscript requires a new review, not merely a refreshed hash.

The selection checker can reject absent discovery records, URL-only pseudo-searches, disconnected roles, unlocated claims and stale text. It cannot discover a question not recorded anywhere or judge the intellectual quality of a gap. Test these explicit invariants; evaluate completeness and mathematical meaning by source-based reading.
