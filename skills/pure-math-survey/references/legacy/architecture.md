# Architecture and mathematical hierarchy

Plan from the requested reading task, not from a catalog of papers or files. Record the objects, organizing question, principal promises and exclusions. Distinguish principal answers, supporting inputs and background before drafting; a budget cannot silently replace the agreed question with a special case. The architecture note records the central question, audience, selected scope, major sections and their connections. It also records the core routes and selected explanation targets when III is requested. This is one editorial note, not an additional audit database or a mandatory printed chart.

Related material belongs only when it supplies necessary data, an input, a decisive mechanism or a boundary of an asserted relationship. Shared terminology alone does not make another main thread. Use [the Part V guide](part-v-benchmark.md) for purpose-specific, optional samples.

## Three logical levels

**Mathematical perspective.** Identify the article's central question and the selected answers. Definitions, a displayed problem and principal theorems may make the perspective clear without a narrative preamble. Explain significance or a relationship only when it adds mathematical information. A short article can have one thread.

**Major sections.** Make each section a principal unit of the mathematical argument. Group related local results under the question they serve. The architecture note records each section's task and dependencies; printed transitions are not required when these are evident. An independently stored component is not thereby a top-level section.

**Local subsections.** Put technical variants, calculations, examples and subsidiary claims beneath the task they support. Keep genuinely independent major questions as sections. Do not create decorative levels or prescribe a number of sections, themes, paragraphs or headings.

For the introduction, state the setting, necessary definitions and principal conclusions in a mathematically clear order. A sequence of definitions and theorems is fully acceptable and is the fallback when no substantive transition helps. A short narrative or roadmap is optional. Keep nontrivial comparisons or dependencies, but delete sentences merely announcing what comes next. Introduce only the notation needed for the displayed questions and statements.

Reorganize the body at the same time. A section can open directly with a definition, formula, theorem or proof step. Concise and standard may have different printed hierarchies. `\input` boundaries are implementation details. Reread the introduction and actual section content: are the central question, core conclusions and necessary relationships understandable? Check the body's fulfillment of these commitments and all real references. Do not require explanatory prose when ordered mathematics already supplies the organization.

## Part-specific organization

| Part | Mathematical thread in the introduction | Relationship between major sections |
|---|---|---|
| I | Why the objects and questions matter; what a model reveals | The necessary language enables a model, whose structure leads to the general question |
| II | How known conclusions answer the central question; which distinctions matter | Result groups organized by implication, comparison, difference or improvement |
| III | Which large proof ideas resolve which difficulties | Method families divide the work; local bridges serve a route; the outputs close the target |
| IV | Where the theory applies, where it is limited, and what gaps define the frontier | Applications and boundaries lead to selected Problems or precisely delimited directions; progress changes their remaining scope |
| V | What is known overall, why the methods help, and what deserves further study | Necessary language supports selected results and their relationships; method ideas and Problems complete the thematic account |

## I: Foundations and Models

Explain the central objects and a completed core model, construction or tractable case, then show what transfers to the general problem. Supply the definitions and background actually used. Standard deepens foundations and model reasoning; concise preserves the necessary conceptual bridge. Do not require a governing equation or invent an obstacle where the model's reasoning already extends directly. A model here explains an object or question; an application later tests the force of a result.

## II: Results and Relations

Organize core results by the mathematical questions they answer, not author chronology. State compact self-contained theorems with explicit hypotheses, using lists when clearer. Keep each theorem's core conclusion separate from secondary consequences. Cite controlling versions and justify genuine relationships, preferably with precise formulas where suitable. Match objects, assumptions, quantifiers, ranges and conclusions before claiming implication, improvement or equivalence. Prove or cite review-derived comparisons. Give substantive proof ideas at this part's scale; detailed development belongs to III when requested. A concise explanation must not defer all meaning to standard.

## III: Proofs and Methods

Explain each promised core proof family's target, main issue, decisive idea, intermediate outputs, uses of hypotheses and closing inference. Separately develop selected decisive mechanisms: a genuine bottleneck, short bridge or transferable construction can qualify. Standard deepens these arguments and dependencies where helpful. Precisely stated and located background inputs need not be reproved. Record what is imported and what this document promises to explain; do not relabel an unfulfilled central target as background. Review the original architecture against both requested III entries, including their actual input files.

## IV: Applications, Boundaries and Problems

Use the theory through meaningful constructions or applications with checked hypotheses and a derived conclusion. Distinguish failure of an assumption, failure of a method and failure of a conclusion. A counterexample is appropriate only when established, not as a required category. When the agreed core frontier contains authenticated precise questions, give explicit Problem statements, then discuss verified progress, remaining ranges, importance and evidenced relationships. Keep a direction or a refuted formulation distinct; no Problem is compulsory when none is justified. Standard adds depth to these applications and discussions; concise retains the core Problems, not merely a promise that they appear elsewhere.

## V: An independently organized thematic review

Choose V's own central question and core. Use verified research and, where helpful, other parts' exposition; neither I–IV's prior existence nor a particular generation order is required. The common core of V's editions is not the union of all I–IV nodes.

Explain the problem's background and importance, necessary objects and notation, accurate main conclusions and their relationships, ideas behind the methods, and selected worthwhile Problems or directions. Independent readability means that the chosen reader can understand these statements and conditions within V. It does not mean teaching an entire prerequisite course or reproducing I's models.

For concise, select principal results and necessary support, merge repeated explanations, combine local sections and drop secondary calculations or branches. Explain a method's idea, job and connection to the conclusion, through short prose, a formula-based argument or a useful list. Retain a short derivation only when it materially clarifies the thread. Do not automatically import III's complete proof families or technical lemma inventory. Do not shrink fonts or margins or erase conditions to obtain brevity.

Standard deepens background relationships, comparisons, key method ideas and the meaning and progress of the same core Problems. Useful short derivations or examples may be added, but detailed proof obligations arise only from the actual request and declared promises, not V's part number.

The two V templates place necessary definitions and principal statements in the introduction, then develop an independently useful reduction, a technical input, closure and a relevant boundary. Recall wrappers are optional, not immediate duplicates or substitutes for body results. These are removable task prompts, not a fixed section taxonomy. Replace them with mathematical titles and, where clearer, interweave results and method ideas. Each template allows a definition-and-theorem introduction without compulsory transitions or a section roadmap. Explain only the relationships needed to interpret the selected results. Concise includes its own selection and merging decisions; standard specifies where deeper understanding is added.

Give V one abstract, introduction, notation system, contents, numbering and bibliography. Reuse one canonical body per shared or repeated full statement; useful local restatements may input that same body with a valid recall wrapper. Reuse, compress, rewrite or reorganize prose freely. A reference to an unproduced companion must not carry necessary assumptions. Reuse does not certify integration quality, imply a generation order or reveal token consumption.

## Shared facts and different selections

A mapped node has one mathematical body and a part I–IV owner describing its role, even when it appears only in V. Ownership creates no obligation to produce that standalone part. V's two editions may independently give a full statement, an accurately located and self-sufficient reference, or a justified omission outside their core. I–IV standard retains its full-statement rule. Use the [format reference](records-and-delivery.md) rather than expanding the schema.

Shared statements and Problems retain their assumptions, source versions and knowledge status. A V Problem selection need not exhaust IV; it must satisfy V's own advertised thread without implying omitted subjects are settled. Repair shared facts once and check actual affected placements. Core selection and reasons for omission require substantive review, not automated coverage scores.

## Principal-conclusion coverage

Before drafting, identify the organizing questions and select the conclusions actually promised by the title, abstract, central questions and planned body. In this same architecture note, record a compact mapping: selected conclusion or decisive boundary; necessary local definitions; introduction label/passage; body label/passage; controlling source/version; full statement or explicitly delimited special case. Reuse source and theorem IDs already present; do not introduce another result registry. An author/keyword/section name is not a mathematical conclusion.

Write the introduction so the declared reader can recover the objects, material conditions, quantifiers, actual conclusion, scope and status without visiting the body. Group actual statements by mathematical relation; add specialized definitions just before their use. Secondary estimates or proof lemmas need not become principal results. Conversely, an independent main thread cannot be omitted because another theorem has already appeared.

After completing the body, check both directions. Inspect the body for principal knowledge absent from the introduction, and inspect the introduction for unfulfilled announcements. Do not retrospectively demote a promised core result to avoid repair. A changed hypothesis, conclusion or status triggers the abstract/introduction/body/canonical-body recheck. Record actual locations in the existing note and the existing `reader_outcomes` evidence.

I introduces the knowledge obtained from its chosen model or construction; it does not invent a research theorem. II gives actual main results and logical relations. III states what the promised methods output and how those outputs reach the target, not just method names. IV states decisive boundaries, known progress and precise remaining targets, distinguishing theorem from conjecture. V gives the selected thematic answers, necessary comparisons, method roles and core questions. Each part and edition is assessed independently.

For V, use [the Part V guide](part-v-benchmark.md). No historical or topical example supplies a binding whole-article organization. Choose samples only for a stated local purpose.

## Result importance relative to the selected question

Record `result_role`, `question_ids`, `selection_reason` and `consumer_ids` in the existing publication map. Keep editorial roles distinct from logical roles in the structure review:

- `principal-answer`: directly answers an organizing question; its actual statement belongs in the opening.
- `essential-bridge`: needed to understand or close the selected answer or precise remaining gap; identify its consumer.
- `boundary-result`: determines a limit of the answer, with its own hypotheses; importance is not automatic.
- `secondary-independent-result`: alternative method or independent result not required by the main chain; justify retained space.
- `background-context`: supplies local language, provenance or an adjacent connection, not another main question.

Beauty and technical difficulty do not make a result principal. An environment does not determine importance. Frontier questions have their own status/disposition, not a result role. Record excluded candidates and reasons in the existing reader evidence. Select the common concise/standard core before proof depth. Every core result and mechanism has a real placement in each requested edition; an unrequested edition is not a completed paired test. Once-used statements may remain inline; canonical bodies are required for actual shared/repeated statements. Delete empty transitions, not local definitions or proof input/output interfaces.
