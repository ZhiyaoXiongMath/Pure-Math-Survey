\section{Mechanism and complete proof of the known result}
For the fixed blocks, let
\[
 Q(x,y)=x^TAx+2x^TBy+y^TCy,\qquad z=x+A^{-1}By.
\]
The inverse exists because $A>0$. Since $A^{-1}$ is symmetric,
\[
 z^TAz=x^TAx+2x^TBy+y^TB^TA^{-1}By.
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:known-square}
 Q(x,y)=z^TAz+y^TSy,\qquad S=C-B^TA^{-1}B.
\end{equation}
The map $(x,y)\mapsto(z,y)$ is invertible, with
$x=z-A^{-1}By$. It preserves whether the pair is nonzero.

If $S>0$, the right side of \eqref{eq:known-square} is positive for every
nonzero pair, proving $M>0$. Conversely, if $M>0$, take any $y\ne0$
and set $x=-A^{-1}By$. This gives $0<Q(x,y)=y^TSy$, hence $S>0$.

For fixed $y$, $z^TAz\geq0$, with equality exactly at $z=0$.
The unique minimizing $x$ is consequently $-A^{-1}By$, and the minimum
is $y^TSy$. This closes the elementary proof of the known principal.

For $A=2$, $B=C=1$, one directly obtains
\[
 Q(x,y)=2(x+y/2)^2+y^2/2.
\]
For $A=-1$, $B=0$, $C=1$, the identity still holds but gives
$Q(x,y)=-x^2+y^2$. Then $S=1>0$ while $M$ is indefinite and the
fixed-$y$ problem is unbounded below.
