# Comparison theorems in Riemannian geometry

Curvature is measured at one point and in one tangent plane. Comparison theory asks how such measurements constrain the separation of geodesics, the size of balls and the geometry of whole manifolds. The first reading here follows seven classical interfaces through the operations that connect them. The [focused question](nodes/N-Q-classical-core.md), [scope](../scope.md) and [coverage record](coverage.json) distinguish this route from the wider project and its unfinished branches. This is the current knowledge map; historical manuscripts and their snapshots are separate records.

## Reader entrance and available operations

Start with smooth manifolds, tangent vectors, linear algebra, multivariable calculus and elementary ODEs. A previous course in connection calculus is not assumed. The [operational entry](nodes/N-D-riemannian.md#operational-entry) explains how a positive definite metric measures lengths and volume, how its metric connection differentiates a moving vector, and how a parallel orthonormal frame reduces that derivative to ordinary component differentiation. The [gradient and Hessian](nodes/N-D-riemannian.md#gradient-and-hessian-as-operations) then turn a scalar function into a vector field and a symmetric bilinear form. For Euclidean distance, the radial Hessian is zero and every perpendicular direction has value \(1/r\); this is the first sign check, not a prerequisite theorem to memorize.

We import existence of the Levi-Civita connection and geodesics, Gauss lemma, the completeness/minimization theorem, the cut-locus theorem and the stated triangle gluing input. Their exact uses are identified in the [geometric interfaces](nodes/N-M-geometric-inputs.md#scope-of-the-imported-geometry) and the relevant mechanisms. We derive the connecting calculations. In particular, saying “use polar coordinates” does not replace finding their differential or volume density.

The [radial curvature operator](nodes/N-D-curvature.md#the-radial-curvature-operator) measures how two covariant derivatives fail to commute. On the plane directions perpendicular to a unit vector \(T\), its quadratic form records sectional curvature and its trace is \(\operatorname{Ric}(T,T)\). This is the distinction between the directional and volume routes below.

Fix a base point \(p\), a unit vector \(v\), and the radial geodesic \(\gamma_v(t)=\exp_p(tv)\). Varying \(v\) produces a perpendicular Jacobi field \(J\). In a parallel frame its equation is an ordinary matrix ODE. The [radial variation calculation](nodes/N-D-jacobi.md#radial-variation-and-the-exponential-differential) constructs the fundamental matrix \(A_v\), with \(A_v(0)=0,\ A_v'(0)=I\), and proves

\[
d\Phi_{(t,v)}(w)=A_v(t)w,\qquad
d(\exp_p)_{tv}(w)=t^{-1}A_v(t)w \quad(w\perp v).
\]

The factor \(t^{-1}\) distinguishes an angular variation from a vector at \(tv\). Keep track of this before using either equation. The [model calculation](nodes/N-D-jacobi.md#one-complete-model-calculation) gives \(A_v=s_kI\) in constant curvature and fixes all comparison signs. Here \(s_k\), \(c_k=s_k'/s_k\) and the first model zero \(D_k\) have the conventions in [the model definition](nodes/N-D-model.md).

## Ricci lower bounds to ball volume

The fast route has six steps. Each link opens the calculation that justifies the transition.

1. Before the cut time, \(A_v\) is invertible. A vector \(X\) at \(\gamma_v(t)\) corresponds to the initial angular vector \(A_v(t)^{-1}X\). Torsion freeness therefore gives \(\nabla_X\nabla r=A_v'A_v^{-1}X\) for \(X\perp\dot\gamma_v\). Thus \(S=A_v'A_v^{-1}\) is the distance Hessian as an endomorphism and \(m=\operatorname{tr}S=\Delta r\); the radial Hessian is zero. See [current vectors and the distance Hessian](nodes/N-M-geometric-inputs.md#current-vectors-and-the-distance-hessian).
2. Differentiating \(S\) yields \(S'+S^2+R_{\dot\gamma}=0\). Its trace and \(\operatorname{tr}S^2\ge m^2/(n-1)\) give a scalar inequality for \(m\) under \(\operatorname{Ric}\ge(n-1)kg\). The lost directional information explains why this route gives a Laplacian bound, not a Hessian bound. See [the controlled trace](nodes/N-M-trace.md#why-the-trace-is-the-controlled-quantity).
3. Both scalar comparison solutions have a pole at zero. Subtracting them and multiplying by an integrating factor whose size is \(Ct^2\) makes the initial boundary term tend to zero. This proves \(m\le(n-1)c_k\) on \(0<t<\min\{\tau(v),D_k\}\), the [Laplacian comparison interface](nodes/N-R-laplacian.md). See [the singular calculation](nodes/N-M-trace.md#singular-scalar-comparison-by-an-integrating-factor).
4. The radial/angular metric has block matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(1,A_v^TA_v)\), so its density is \(|\det A_v|\). Its sign is positive before the first conjugate point, hence \(j=\det A_v\), and \((\log j)'=m\). Consequently \(j/s_k^{n-1}\) decreases from \(1\). See [the density construction](nodes/N-D-jacobi.md#from-the-radial-differential-to-volume-density) and [the normalized density](nodes/N-M-trace.md#normalized-density-and-the-small-radius-limit).
5. The exponential map is one-to-one on the uncut domain, and the omitted cut locus has volume zero. This identifies the ball integral with \(t<\min\{r,\tau(v)\}\). Extending the density by zero at cut time makes a downward jump, so the normalized density still decreases. Angular averaging preserves this order, and dominated convergence gives initial value \(1\). See [the ball substitution](nodes/N-M-cut-integration.md#the-uncut-domain-and-the-ball-integral) and [angular averaging](nodes/N-M-cut-integration.md#zero-extension-and-angular-averaging).
6. The ball-volume ratio is the positive weighted average of that decreasing angular ratio, with weight \(s_k^{n-1}\). The calculation \(F'=(w/W)(q-F)\le0\), together with local absolute continuity, gives monotonicity for every pair of radii. See [the averaging proof](nodes/N-M-cut-integration.md#a-positive-weighted-average). This proves [Bishop–Gromov](nodes/N-R-bishop-gromov.md). If \(k>0\), the independent Myers argument bounds the diameter by \(D_k\); the terminal distance sphere is contained in the cut locus and has zero volume. The [endpoint argument](nodes/N-M-cut-integration.md#the-positive-curvature-endpoint) extends the volume ratio without dividing by \(s_k(D_k)=0\).

One immediate use is \(\operatorname{Vol}B(p,2r)\le2^n\operatorname{Vol}B(p,r)\) when \(\operatorname{Ric}\ge0\). A relative lower estimate needs the volume of a larger ball as input; no absolute noncollapse follows. The [collapsing cylinders](nodes/N-E-collapse.md) compute the obstruction. Changing a Ricci lower bound to an upper bound does not reverse this proof: the uncontrolled traceless part of \(S\) has the wrong sign already in [the trace equation](nodes/N-M-trace.md#what-the-hypotheses-do-not-give).

## Sectional bounds to Jacobi length and Hessian

A sectional bound controls the full symmetric radial curvature operator. The [Wronskian and Riccati calculations](nodes/N-M-riccati.md#symmetry-and-the-riccati-equation) identify the operator that can be compared. The [matrix-order argument](nodes/N-M-riccati.md#matrix-comparison-and-its-imported-singular-boundary) explains why a curvature lower bound yields \(S\le c_kI\); the singular initial comparison is a precisely imported theorem. Combining this with the current-vector calculation gives [Hessian comparison](nodes/N-R-hessian.md), including the zero radial direction.

For a normal field \(J=A_vw\ne0\), differentiate \(\log(|J|/(|w|s_k))\). The bound on \(S\) makes this derivative nonpositive, and the limit at zero is \(1\). This gives [Rauch comparison](nodes/N-R-rauch.md). A sectional upper bound reverses the inequality; its lower length estimate excludes an earlier zero of \(A_v\) by continuity. The [length and continuation calculation](nodes/N-M-riccati.md#jacobi-length-and-exponential-map-use) writes out both steps and translates angular fields into actual curve speeds. The lower-curvature version must stop before the actual first conjugate point; [the round-sphere test](nodes/N-E-rauch-cutoff.md) shows why. Cut time and conjugate time play different roles: a Jacobi field can be followed beyond a cut point, while the smooth global distance Hessian cannot.

The Riccati route treats all perpendicular directions together and then takes a trace when needed. For a single endpoint Hessian bound there is also an [index-form calculation](nodes/N-D-index.md#an-endpoint-field-calculation): compare the endpoint Jacobi field with \(s_k(t)E(t)/s_k(L)\), integrate \((s_ks_k')'\), and obtain \(c_k(L)|X|^2\). This is a concrete alternative route to the same lower-sectional-curvature Hessian estimate. It neither supplies the cut-locus substitution nor removes the singular/domain checks in the Riccati route.

## Myers from endpoint fields to global finiteness

With \(\operatorname{Ric}\ge(n-1)kg\), \(k>0\), test a minimizing segment of length \(L\) by \(V_i(t)=\sin(\pi t/L)E_i(t)\). The sine vanishes at both endpoints and has the simplest Dirichlet frequency. Summing the index forms uses precisely the Ricci trace and yields

\[
\sum_i I(V_i,V_i)\le\frac{(n-1)L}{2}\bigl((\pi/L)^2-k\bigr).
\]

If \(L>D_k\), this is negative, contrary to minimizing second variation. The [calculation and global steps](nodes/N-M-myers.md#the-sine-field-calculation) separate this contradiction from completeness, compactness and the lift to the universal cover. They give [Bonnet–Myers](nodes/N-R-myers.md), including finiteness of the fundamental group. The positive constant must be uniform; [completeness and scaling tests](nodes/N-E-completeness.md) prevent weaker hypotheses from being substituted silently.

## Hadamard from local invertibility to a global cover

When sectional curvature is nonpositive, the Jacobi equation makes \(|J|^2\) convex for point-centered normal fields. There is no nontrivial second zero. Adding the radial component in the [differential calculation](nodes/N-M-hadamard.md#the-radial-and-angular-parts-of-the-differential) proves that the exponential map is a local diffeomorphism. This alone does not make it globally injective. The [pullback metric](nodes/N-M-hadamard.md#completeness-of-the-pullback-metric) on \(T_pM\) has entire radial lines as all its geodesics from zero; the one-point completeness criterion therefore makes it complete. The [evenly covered ball construction](nodes/N-M-hadamard.md#why-the-local-isometry-is-a-covering) now applies. Simple connectivity gives [one sheet and unique minimizing geodesics](nodes/N-M-hadamard.md#one-sheet-and-unique-minimizing-geodesics), proving [Cartan–Hadamard](nodes/N-R-hadamard.md). The [flat cylinder](nodes/N-E-cylinder.md) keeps the covering conclusion and disproves global injectivity when simple connectivity is removed.

## Toponogov from local lengths to global triangles

First fix the comparison experiment. Equal three side lengths compare angles and internal cross-distances in one direction; equal two side lengths and their included angle compare the opposite side in the other direction. The [triangle definition](nodes/N-D-triangle.md) specifies the matching points. [Toponogov](nodes/N-R-toponogov.md) uses chosen minimizing sides, strict triangle inequalities and perimeter \(<2D_k\); it does not require that all of \(M\) lie in one normal neighborhood.

The [comparison map](nodes/N-M-toponogov.md#the-comparison-map-on-a-common-normal-domain) uses the exponential differential on a common small normal domain. Its [radial/angular speed calculation and chord containment](nodes/N-M-toponogov.md#from-angular-and-radial-speeds-to-length) give a local length comparison. The monotone model cosine law converts it into local angle comparison. An [explicit gluing input](nodes/N-M-toponogov.md#the-exact-gluing-and-globalization-inputs) turns a failed global triangle into a smaller failed triangle. Iteration contracts perimeters by \(4/5\); the [\(9/5\) bound on consecutive vertex displacements](nodes/N-M-toponogov.md#shrinking-hinges-and-the-cauchy-limit) sums to a Cauchy tail bounded by \(9P_0(4/5)^n\). Completeness supplies a limit. A [normal radius uniform for nearby vertices](nodes/N-M-toponogov.md#a-uniform-local-neighborhood-for-moving-centers) then gives the local contradiction. Finally, the [auxiliary-perimeter check](nodes/N-M-toponogov.md#global-hinge-and-chord-comparison) allows the angle-to-chord lemma to be applied to all required connecting hinges. These are the additional steps needed after Rauch. A [flat-torus example](nodes/N-E-global-reverse.md) shows that a sectional upper bound cannot simply reverse this global theorem.

## Wider branches and evidence status

The seven interfaces do not exhaust comparison geometry. [Günther lower volume](nodes/N-R-gunther.md) needs an injectivity-radius restriction because zero extension would destroy the required lower density bound. [Maximal model-volume rigidity](nodes/N-R-volume-rigidity.md) is an open-ball conclusion with specified equality data; [local equality examples](nodes/N-E-local-equality.md) separate it from ambient rigidity. The existing [Cheng](nodes/N-R-cheng.md) and [splitting](nodes/N-R-splitting.md) branches use [support functions](nodes/N-M-support.md) and explicitly imported analytic regularity. They remain in the broader map without being represented as complete foundational proofs in the seven-interface route.

The [unassessed branches](nodes/N-B-unassessed.md) include full focal/tube comparison, Dirichlet eigenvalue comparison, Lichnerowicz–Obata, Lévy–Gromov, topology and quantitative rigidity. Weighted, integral, synthetic and non-Riemannian theories need their own conventions. These are literature-incorporation tasks, not claims that the mathematics is open. The broader equality and triangle-endpoint gaps retain their existing status in [coverage](coverage.json).
