# Riemannian comparison theorems: knowledge scope

This project began as a knowledge-only request on 2026-10-12. A later request produced a minimal English survey for a first-year doctoral student who has studied smooth manifolds; its historical outputs and snapshots retain their own evidence. This public r3 example starts from the accepted S02 knowledge snapshot and authors two new outputs: a seven-branch Minimal organized into local question units, and a Lecture deriving the Ricci-to-ball-volume route in full detail while retaining all seven complete principal statements and the other branches at explicitly bounded mechanism depth. The knowledge investigation remains broader than the seven-interface first reading. Mathematical records are in English and user-facing discussion is in Chinese.

## Objects and questions

The ambient objects are smooth, connected Riemannian manifolds (M^n,g), n >= 2, without boundary. Completeness is imposed in each global result, not inferred for all local statements. The metric and base point are fixed when a comparison is made. Sectional curvature K and the Ricci tensor Ric are intrinsic data; normal coordinates, parallel frames and polar coordinates are representatives used in proofs, not additional geometric assumptions.

The main targets are inequalities for normal Jacobi fields, the Hessian and Laplacian of r_p=d(p,·), polar volume densities, volumes of concentric balls, diameter, triangle sides and angles; and the rigidity/topological consequences justified by those estimates. The model is the simply connected n-dimensional space form of sectional curvature k. The convention Ric >= (n-1)k g is fixed. Ordered quantifiers, radial versus global curvature assumptions, admissible radii and dependencies on n,k,p are stated separately in every formal node.

## Initial investigation and continuing perimeter

The initial checked core follows both classical routes: Rauch and Riccati/Hessian comparison under sectional bounds; traced Riccati/Laplacian and Bishop–Gromov comparison under Ricci lower bounds; Bonnet–Myers, Cartan–Hadamard, and Toponogov. Model calculations, cut/conjugate distinctions and failures of reverse implications are part of this core. Gunther lower-volume comparison and rigidity/splitting/spectral comparisons are investigated as an omission challenge and are either incorporated with evidence or explicitly left as named, unassessed branches.

The broad project remains comparison theory in smooth Riemannian geometry. This first population is not an exhaustive classification. Precise outstanding branches are recorded in discovery and coverage, and are not labelled open mathematical problems merely because they have not yet been read.

Synthetic Alexandrov/CAT/RCD theories, weighted/Bakry–Emery comparison, integral curvature bounds, Lorentzian or Finsler analogues, Ricci-flow pinching, and submanifold/tube comparison are neighboring extensions requiring separate conventions. They are recorded as later branches, with no claim that classical hypotheses automatically transfer. No original theorem or originality claim is requested.

## Reader and evidence

Assumed for the current first reading: multivariable calculus, linear algebra, elementary ODEs and smooth manifolds. Refreshable: tangent/vector bundle notation, symmetric operators, trace/determinant and basic topology. Bridge-required: metric/connection formalism, geodesic variation, curvature, Jacobi tensors, matrix Riccati comparison, cut-locus integration and globalization of triangle comparison. N-D-riemannian, N-D-curvature and N-M-geometric-inputs supply the operational entry. The focused question and overview locate the current reading tasks; any future output must derive its own obligations from this knowledge and its requested scope.

Statements are supported by actual source-section readings or explicit local arguments. Historical provenance is separated from the controlling exposition. Research-agent assistance does not count as independent peer review. Structural validation and immutable snapshots verify records and bytes, not mathematical truth.
