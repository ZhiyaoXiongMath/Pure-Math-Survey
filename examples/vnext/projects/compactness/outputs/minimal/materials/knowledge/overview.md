# Why an estimate is not yet a closedness argument

The selected problem starts with solutions already in hand. It does not claim to
produce them. A uniform C^{2,alpha} estimate first supplies a C^{2,beta} convergent
subsequence, for beta<alpha: [[N-compactness]], “Diagonal extraction” and “The strong
topology,” give the actual derivative and interpolation argument. Uniform convergence
alone would not yet justify a nonlinear operator using second derivatives.

Two further premises are joint, not competing methods. Continuity of the matrix map
A in this strong topology gives uniform matrix convergence. A single positive lower
bound delta survives the limit by testing all unit vectors; [[N-margin]] explains the
same control as a uniform inverse bound. Only after this step is the limit in the
domain on which F is continuous. [[N-main]], “Admissibility and passage through the
equation,” then passes F(t_j,u_j)=0 to F(t_infty,u_infty)=0.

This project also compares two margin formulations and asks how much convergence
is obtained. The oscillatory example in [[N-boundary]] rules out C^{2,alpha} convergence;
u_j=1/j with A(u)=u I shows why separate positivity is not a uniform margin.
Neither example denies the stated conditional theorem. No arbitrary nonlinear
existence, uniform estimate from a PDE, smoothness upgrade or whole-sequence convergence
is asserted. The project map can support several article choices without being copied
as a common introduction or becoming a second canonical theorem store.
