Theorem 1 uses a seminormed nonnegative cone M+ in A0, fixed continuous injection A0->A1 and compact gauge-bounded inclusion M+->A0. Its gauge is positive definite and positively degree-one homogeneous; no triangle inequality or closure under addition/subtraction is assumed. Bounds are Lp in the gauge and Lq in the A1-valued distributional derivative. The permitted exponents are q>1 with any 1<=p<=infinity, or q=1 with finite p. The conclusion is compactness in Lp(A0); no limit-in-cone conclusion is asserted. Remark 2 specifically excludes (infinity,1).

Lemma 1 is a two-point Ehrling estimate: ||u-v||A0<=eta([u]+[v])+C_eta||u-v||A1. Its proof normalizes both points by [u]+[v], never by a gauge of u-v. Both normalized sequences stay in the cone's bounded gauge set; compactness and injectivity identify their limits. This is a genuine difference from using a linear X norm on u-v.

Scoped proof-reading concern: at p.6, the argument following (2.8) introduces n_k=n(t,k) depending on t and then treats the integral of [u_{n_k}(t)]^p as if it were bounded by the uniform fixed-index integral estimate (2.7). That dependence needs justification not supplied in the passage. At p.8, the description of an infinite nested-set intersection also needs the usual diagonal-subsequence formulation. We record these as unresolved justifications of the cited proof passage, not as a claim that Theorem 1 or Lemma 2 is false. This project does not use those passages as proof input.

Instead N-cone-mechanism gives a complete local reconstruction of the needed weak-space compactness: W11 supplies an A1 supremum bound, gauge truncation supplies small-measure exceptional sets, the bounded amplitude makes their L1 tails small, compact convex hulls give compact interval integrals, and Simon's criterion gives weak-space compactness. The verified two-point Ehrling inequality transfers it to A0. The local proof includes all finite-p exponents and the p=infinity representative issue. Therefore no unchecked source proof is silently certified.

Section 4 states a special reduction through an injective positively homogeneous K of degree alpha>=1 and the gauge [v]=||K^{-1}v||^alpha. This is not an arbitrary equivalence between all cones and all nonlinear maps. The project makes strong measurability of the inverse lift explicit and calculates its L1 bound. The stronger space A0 is chosen equal to A1=E2 in this special comparison, so no weak/strong norm mismatch is hidden. General cones need not come with such a parametrization, and Maitre itself does not require injectivity/homogeneity.
