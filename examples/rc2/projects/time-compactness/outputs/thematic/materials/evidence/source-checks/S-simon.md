Read on 2026-10-12 by the authoring agent. Controlling object is the journal text with printed pp.65--96 and DOI 10.1007/BF01762360, not a later reformulation. The Springer DOI page explicitly gives December 1986 and volume 146, pp.65--96; Maitre and Barrett--Suli cite the same text as 1987. We preserve the publisher's calendar label in bibliography.year and record the discrepancy here; theorem numbering and page locators, not the year alone, identify the source. No unresolved competing mathematical text was found.

Access: original scan at https://www.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~kaplicky/pages/pages/2012z/b2.pdf (17 scanned two-page sheets, rotated); searchable transcription of the same original at https://scispace.com/pdf/compact-sets-in-the-spacel-p-o-t-b-272xozldtk.pdf. The PDF was downloaded into transient scratch as compactness-sources/simon.pdf; its actual hash is in the source record. It is not redistributed in the KB. Searchable OCR mangles inequalities, so the scan of printed pp.72--73, 84--85 and 94--95 was opened visually. In particular Theorem 5 allows 1<=p<=infinity; OCR's strict inequality is not trusted.

Sections actually read: introductory problem and compactness mechanisms; Section 3, Theorem 1 and proof, pp.71--73; Section 6, Theorem 3 and its proof, p.80; Section 8, Theorem 5, Lemmas 8--9 and Corollary 4, pp.84--86; Section 11, Proposition 3, p.94, with the scaled bump context. Sections on fractional regularity/interpolation were only navigated for scope and are not certified here.

Theorem 1 uses relative compactness of every interior interval integral and uniform translation convergence on (0,T-h). For p=infinity the conclusion is compactness of continuous representatives, not a characterization of arbitrary L-infinity compact sets: a singleton discontinuous function is compact in L-infinity yet fails the translation condition. The right-mean proof first gives compactness on (0,T-a); its time-reversed argument supplies the other endpoint. Merely using centered averages would leave an endpoint gap (Remark 3.4).

Theorem 5 fixes Banach embeddings X->B->Y with X->B compact. F is bounded in Lp(X), and translations tend to zero uniformly in Lp(Y). The resulting compactness is Lp(B), or C for p=infinity. Lemma 8 supplies, for each eta>0, a constant C_eta with ||z||B<=eta||z||X+C_eta||z||Y for z in X. Lemma 9 upgrades Y-compactness using this estimate. No reflexivity hypothesis occurs. The proof invokes Theorem 1 in Y. Corollary 4 separately permits derivatives in L1(Y) when p<infinity, but requires derivative exponent r>1 for the C conclusion with L-infinity(X) boundedness.

Proposition 3 warns precisely against (p,q)=(infinity,1). The project uses its own explicit piecewise-linear ramp, not an unattributed transcription of Simon's bump: the ramp has derivative L1 norm one, uniform sup bound one, and pointwise discontinuous limit. Its argument is fully present in N-endpoint.

Normalization: local tau_h f(t)=f(t+h), h>0; translation domains are (0,T-h). E1,E2 and K used elsewhere avoid conflating Simon's space B with Maitre's operator B. Imported theorem bodies are mathematical restatements with explicit fixed T and quantifiers. These readings establish support, not an independent verification of every argument in the 32-page paper.
